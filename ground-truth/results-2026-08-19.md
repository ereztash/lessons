> Run 2026-08-19. n=10. Rubric v1.1 (`rubric.md`), labels (`labels-2026-08-19.json`),
> harness (`scripts/score-classifier.py`). Reproduce with:
> `python3 scripts/score-classifier.py ground-truth/labels-2026-08-19.json`

**The classifier orders repos well and calibrates them badly.** ρ=0.77 says the ranking is
broadly right. 20% exact agreement with every single error in the same direction says the whole
scale is shifted up by about one tier. That is a bias, not noise — with 8 errors, a fair coin
would produce an all-one-direction result 1 time in 128.

## 3. Why it fails, mechanically

Of the 8 mis-rated repos, **8 have no external consumer and 8 have not been touched in 30+ days.**
F1–F4 measures neither. That is the entire error, and it is not a tuning problem.

F1–F4 asks: *does this repo have a production dependency, a human commit, a PR, and docs?* Those
were meaningful proxies for commitment when producing them cost a developer real time. In an
AI-paired portfolio they cost an afternoon. `MATI` acquired all four in eleven hours. The features
measure **apparatus**, and apparatus has become cheap; what stayed expensive is a consumer and a
reason to return.

The classifier is a 2025-era instrument reading 2026-era repos.

## 4. The out-of-time test (contamination-proof)

The n=25 scan ran 2026-05-12. Today is 99 days later, so its predictions can be scored against
events that had not happened when it made them. No labelling judgment is involved.

Six scanned repos are locally checkable. Commits landed since 2026-05-12:

| Repo | May 2026 verdict | Commits since | Outcome |
|---|---|---|---|
| ampaign-craft | Tier A, "healthy (most active repo in portfolio)", 2d dormant | **0** on `main` | not borne out (77 branches unobserved) |
| COR-SYS | Tier A, "healthy (active…)", 33d dormant | **0** | not borne out |
| ground-state-protocol | Tier A, "healthy (most vitally active)", 6d dormant | **9** (to 2026-05-19, then stopped) | briefly borne out |
| brain-healer-hub | Tier B, 39d dormant | 0 | consistent |
| Benchmark.ATS | Tier B, 175d dormant | 0 | consistent |
| All_Erez-s_Connections | Tier B, 41d dormant | 0 | consistent |

Of the three repos the scan called healthy or active, **one produced a further week of work and
none is active today.** Meanwhile the two repos that *are* active on 2026-08-19 — MATI and
anti-silo — did not exist in the scan and could not have been predicted by it.

The scan's dormancy column was accurate, but dormancy is an observation, not a prediction. Its
"healthy" label carried no predictive signal on this sample.

## 5. What follows

1. **Do not delete the classifier. Re-anchor it.** ρ=0.77 says the ordering is real. The error
   analysis points at one missing thing: **an external-consumer feature (F5) plus a recency term.**
   Every one of the 8 errors lacks both, and F1–F4 measures neither.

   **This set cannot validate that fix.** `serving ∧ current` separates the two A's perfectly here
   — but only because ground-truth A is *defined* as `serving ∧ current ∧ executable`. Citing that
   as evidence for F5 would be circular. What this set does support is the negative claim: F1–F4
   carries no information about whether anything consumes the repo, and that omission accounts for
   every error. Validating F5 needs either a fresh set labelled on a different criterion, or a
   prospective test — score the portfolio with F5 today, and check in 90 days which repos advanced.
2. **Every tier number in this repo is provisional until re-scored.** `research/repo-index.md`,
   `research/portfolio-scan/26-repos.md` and the n=30 rescan all carry uncorrected tiers, and the
   measured bias is +1 for 8 of 10 checkable cases. They are being read as verdicts.
3. **The playbooks inherit the bias.** `four-feature-tier-classifier.md` sells this instrument at
   $29–$59 with a claimed 1–3 hours saved per portfolio review. It ships with no accuracy figure.
   Until F5 lands, its claim should read "orders a portfolio; over-rates roughly one tier" — which
   is honest and still useful, rather than silent.
4. **Class C/D discrimination is untested.** All 10 repos here are executable and none is inert.
   The 15 Tier-C/D repos from the n=25 scan are outside session scope; that half of the classifier
   has still never been measured.
5. **This is a self-audit, not a validation.** Same session, single labeller, not blind
   (`rubric.md` §5.1). The result is strong enough to act on and weak enough that a second labeller
   applying `rubric.md` §3 without seeing the tiers is the obvious next step.

## 6. What this does not show

It does not show that the ground-truth labels are correct — only that they were derived by a
stated rule from signals the classifier cannot see. It does not show F1–F4 is worthless; ρ=0.77
is a real ordering signal. And it says nothing about the 15 repos outside session scope, where the
classifier's C/D behaviour lives.
