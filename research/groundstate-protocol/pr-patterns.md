### Template-emergence timeline

- **PR#1 -> #7 (April 14-19):** mixed shapes — short bullets or empty. No consistent template.
- **PR#2 (April 14):** the first long-body PR uses an English `## Summary / Key Changes / Notable Implementation Details` template. This is *not* reused.
- **PR#9 -> #10 (May 6):** a *new* Hebrew RTL template stabilizes — `## האסטרטגיה / ## מה השתנה / ## בדיקות (checkbox list) / ## דגלים (flags list)`. Both PRs of this template format share: a research-findings table near the top, a bulleted change list, a 'tests' checklist with checkboxes, and a 'flags' list naming the placeholders still in code. The footer is `_Generated by [Claude Code](https://claude.ai/code/session_*)_` in both.

The template **emerged at PR#9** (39m 30s TTM) and was reused at PR#10 (50m 13s TTM). Both share the longest TTMs in the repo. The template is Hebrew-first because the audience is Hebrew-first.

## Inferred rules (for cross-repo synthesis)

1. **PRs are notarization (median 9s TTM) — except when they carry research-grade bodies.** The two PRs with long Hebrew-templated bodies (#9: 39m 30s, #10: 50m 13s) are also the *only* two with TTM > 2 minutes. Long bodies buy human pause time *and* attract Codex review.
2. **Hebrew RTL templates stabilize at PR#9.** The template `## האסטרטגיה / ## מה השתנה / ## בדיקות / ## דגלים + _Generated by Claude Code_ footer` is reused at PR#10 verbatim. Two-PR pattern is enough to call it the convention.
3. **Branch reuse signals sprint continuity.** `landing-page-redesign-i1Mwg` carried 5 PRs in 5 hours on Apr 19. `audit-landing-page-OR0tf` carried 3 PRs in 2 hours on May 6. The branch is the *sprint container*; the PR is the *commit checkpoint*.
4. **Codex review surfaces are operative.** PR#10 explicitly cites 'Codex review on PR #10 caught a real P1' as the trigger for the fix commit. groundstate-protocol shows the **Claude-writes -> Codex-reviews -> Claude-fixes** loop running successfully in production.
5. **No Conventional Commits, no scope prefixes — descriptive imperatives only.** Unlike cor-sys (where `feat(scope):` stabilizes by Mar 27), groundstate-protocol commits use long descriptive subjects like 'Reframe Hero and Problem around the hidden-discipline flip' or 'Apply neurostorytelling pass to all landing copy'. This may reflect the editorial nature of the work — the commit subject *is* the story of the change.
